About YourSnowCalculator.com

YourSnowCalculator.com was built to answer three questions that come up every winter across the United States: will school close for snow, how much water is actually in that snowfall, and is a roof carrying a dangerous amount of weight.

Rather than one narrow tool, the site combines three focused calculators — a snow day odds estimator, a snowfall unit and water-equivalent converter, and a roof snow load checker — each calibrated to reflect real regional differences across U.S. states and climate zones, since a snowfall total means something very different in Minnesota than it does in Georgia.

How the calculators are built

The snow day estimator uses regional closure thresholds drawn from well-documented patterns in how different climate zones respond to snowfall, combined with forecast temperature and storm timing. The snowfall converter uses standard snow-density ranges to translate raw depth into liquid water equivalent. The roof load calculator applies commonly used snow-density-to-weight conversions alongside regional building code ground snow load values.

All three tools are estimates meant to inform, not replace, official sources: your school district's own announcements, and a licensed structural engineer for any real roof safety concern.

A note on accuracy

Snow forecasting and school closure decisions involve local judgment calls that no calculator can fully capture — road conditions, staffing, and even neighboring district decisions all play a role. We built this site to be a useful, honest starting point, not a guarantee.
